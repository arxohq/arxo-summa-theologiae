# II–II, question 64, article 7: argument reconstruction pilot

This is an experimental, article-local reconstruction of Aquinas's argument about killing in self-defense. The source files beside this README are immutable inputs pinned by `source-pin.json`; `model/00-package.law` declares named source anchors and attaches them through model-level `@source` metadata. This is source provenance in the model; the native proof documents currently expose empty `sourceAnchors`, so this does not claim native-proof anchoring. A `source_principle` is an assumption transcribed from the pinned text, not an independently proven philosophical truth.

The model begins with the objection in arg. 1 and the scope clarification in ad 1. It distinguishes intending the assailant's death as a means to escape one's own death from intending one's own preservation while the assailant's death occurs as an explicitly unintended effect. The source premise and the death-as-means fact remain declared inputs. A conservative lexical bridge derives intended death from death intended as a means; a conflicting explicit negative remains `BOTH`, and unknown intention is not treated as a negative. The rule does not prove Augustine's principle or establish that the reply defeats the objection.

The respondeo branches now emit conditional relation heads for `defensio_licita`, `defensio_illicita`, and `occisio_intenta_illicita`. Tests never assert these conclusions. The first two depend on the external judgment `vis_moderata`; without an adjudicated answer the query should require judgment, while conflicting positive and negative judgments remain `BOTH`; under §§65–66 that status makes both downstream rule bodies dead, so their conclusions remain `NEITHER` with `COMPUTED`. The intended-killing branch separately accounts for public authority, common-good orientation, and private passion. Every permission branch requires an explicit `self_defense_act`; the public-authority/common-good route additionally requires the same external `vis_moderata` judgment. These are executable conditional chains, not full theological or moral proofs.

The remaining objections and replies are inventoried in `reconstruction.json`. Implemented intermediates preserve the ad 3 distinction between clerical irregularity and moral culpability, keep ad 4's necessity comparison in separate branches, and scope ad 5's cited prohibition to vindictive retaliation. The sed contra's comparison between defense of a house and defense of life remains an open bridge. No finite chain is claimed to prove universal absence of regress.

The source is pinned to the source-reader commit and hashes in the project-level `source-pin.json`.
